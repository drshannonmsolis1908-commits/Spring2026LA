\begin{problem}
Find the matrix for reflection across the $x$-axis.
\end{problem}

A reflection across the $x$-axis keeps the $x$-coordinate the same and changes the sign of the $y$-coordinate. Thus,
\[
(x,y)\mapsto (x,-y).
\]

The matrix is
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0\\
0 & -1
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

\begin{problem}
Find the matrix for rotation counterclockwise by $60^\circ$.
\end{problem}

The standard rotation matrix is
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta & -\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta & \cos\theta
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

For $\theta=60^\circ$:
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos 60^\circ & -\sin 60^\circ\\
\sin 60^\circ & \cos 60^\circ
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Using
\[
\cos 60^\circ=\frac12, \quad \sin 60^\circ=\frac{\sqrt3}{2},
\]
we get
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
\frac12 & -\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\\
\frac{\sqrt3}{2} & \frac12
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

\begin{problem}
Find the matrix for reflection across the line making a $60^\circ$ angle with the $x$-axis.
\end{problem}

The formula for reflection across a line at angle $\theta$ is
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos 2\theta & \sin 2\theta\\
\sin 2\theta & -\cos 2\theta
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Here, $\theta=60^\circ$, so $2\theta=120^\circ$. Thus,
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos 120^\circ & \sin 120^\circ\\
\sin 120^\circ & -\cos 120^\circ
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Using
\[
\cos 120^\circ=-\frac12, \quad \sin 120^\circ=\frac{\sqrt3}{2},
\]
we obtain
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
-\frac12 & \frac{\sqrt3}{2}\\
\frac{\sqrt3}{2} & \frac12
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

\begin{problem}
Explain how you developed the matrix.
\end{problem}

I used the standard formula for reflecting across a line through the origin. Since the line makes a $60^\circ$ angle with the $x$-axis, I substituted $\theta=60^\circ$ into the formula. Because the formula uses $2\theta$, I evaluated the trigonometric values at $120^\circ$. After simplifying, I obtained the final matrix.
